# What retail investors want from a market-information site, and what that means for ZinvestIQ

- **Date:** 2026-11-01
- **Why:** The owner's review of the preview (2026-11-01): the site "looks rushed", with information "dumped together"; it does
  not explain how the pieces link, why they help, or what sets ZinvestIQ apart. Long pages need collapsible sections,
  and the colours look artificial. This note collects the evidence the redesign rests on.
- **Caveat:** Much of the published material on this topic comes from vendors: education sellers, design agencies and
  the competitors themselves. Each claim below names its source and how far it can be trusted. No claim here is a
  measured fact about ZinvestIQ's own visitors; the feedback form (plan §3 A) will give us that.

## 1. Who the visitor is (Sri Lanka)

- **Few active share investors.** The CSE's chairman puts active investors at about 50,000–60,000, roughly 0.2% of the
  population, and plans to reach 100,000–150,000 through branches and agents
  ([Echelon](https://echelon.lk/the-cses-mission-turning-citizens-into-shareholders-in-economic-growth/);
  [LNW](https://lankanewsweb.net/archives/289875/cse-expansion-drive-exposes-sri-lankas-missing-market-driven-economic-strategy/)).
  Most potential visitors are therefore **savers, not traders**.
- **Savers hold fixed deposits.** Sri Lankans mainly hold bank and finance-company deposits
  ([FT, 2017, older](https://www.ft.lk/Columnists/Investment-options-beyond-fixed-deposits-and-savings-accounts/4-643362)).
  One-year bills have at times paid more than one-year FDs
  ([Daily Mirror](https://www.dailymirror.lk/business-news/One-year-T-bills-now-offer-higher-yield-than-what-banks-offer-for-12-month-FDs/273-219367)).
  Retail buyers have had limited access to government securities, which brokers are now opening up
  ([CAL press release, 2026](https://www.dailymirror.lk/press_releases/Capital-Alliance-PLC-CAL-Treasuries-facilitates-easy-and-secure-online-investments-in-Treasury-Bills-and-Bonds/335-245197);
  [CBSL FAQ](https://www.cbsl.gov.lk/faq/government-debt-securities)).
- **Financial literacy is improving but uneven.** The Central Bank measures about 58% of adults as financially
  literate, up from 35% in 2014 ([The Morning](https://www.themorning.lk/articles/8KLO0MiNtzYlrdsb3wOq)). A study of
  investors found most had low objective financial literacy
  ([Tennekoon & Liyanage, 2021](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/357741576_Impact_of_Financial_Literacy_Levels_among_Sri_Lankan_Investors_on_Investment_Choices)).

**Implication:** the front door should speak to a saver who knows FDs and is curious about bills, bonds and shares, as
well as to the existing share investor. Education is part of the product, not an appendix.

## 2. What they say they want

- **Confidence, not capital, is the barrier.** In Finimize's survey of about 2,500 retail investors, 23% want to hold
  shares but don't feel knowledgeable enough. More than half cite company fundamentals as a reason for an investment;
  media coverage ranks lowest (6%)
  ([Finimize, Oct 2026](https://finimize.com/business/resources/insights/investing-platforms-this-is-the-knowledge-gap-your-users-wont-admit-to);
  vendor survey of its own community, indicative only).
- **Education at the point of decision.** The same source argues that people rarely visit a separate learning centre,
  so explanations work best next to the number they explain. Plain, unbiased language over attention-driven feeds.
- **Plain language over jargon.** Research is still mostly PDFs and jargon; the opportunity is turning it into plain
  language ([Finimize interview](https://finimize.com/business/resources/insights/how-to-build-a-retail-investor-strategy-insights-from-curation-ceo-nirav-karia);
  opinion). The WEF found retail investors increasingly see crypto as *more understandable* than shares, ETFs and
  bonds, and calls for better education and tools
  ([WEF, Mar 2025](https://weforum.org/press/2025/03/new-research-finds-retail-investing-shift-towards-younger-investors-reshaping-market-trends)).
- **Pictures over statements.** Simply Wall St was founded on the observation that retail investors bought on hype
  because proper research seemed too complicated. Its answer is visual summaries and a deliberate refusal to say what
  to buy ([Startup Daily](https://www.startupdaily.net/advice/fintech-startup-simply-wall-st-pinterest-stocks-designed-novice-gen-y-investors/);
  [company comparison page](https://simplywall.st/vs/simply-wall-street-vs-morningstar); company material).
- **Two kinds of user.** India's two leading research sites split the market: Screener.in is data-first for experienced
  fundamental analysts; Tickertape offers guided analysis for beginners and intermediates
  ([strike.money](https://www.strike.money/reviews/screener); [Finology](https://insider.finology.in/finance/ticker-tape-vs-screener);
  review sites, weak). A site serving both needs a guided top layer and the full data underneath.

## 3. What competes for the same visitor in Sri Lanka

All of the following cover **CSE shares only**; none of the pages reviewed mentions government bonds, treasury bills
or fixed deposits (vendor pages, read 2026-11-01).

| Product | What it offers | How it positions itself | Price |
|---|---|---|---|
| [EquityLK](https://equitylk.lk/) | Fundamentals since 2012, value ranking 0–100, news sentiment, AI reports with a "stance", screener, portfolio, Sinhala | "Institutional-grade insight", one everyday app | Free |
| [CSEdge](https://csedge.lk/en) | **BUY/HOLD/SELL "AI signals"** from community price targets, AI report analysis, screener, Monte Carlo, ownership graphs, trilingual | "The edge is in the data" | Free; Pro Rs 1,000/month or Rs 10,000/year; founding Rs 450/month |
| [AnalytiCAL (CAL)](https://cal.lk/analytical/) | Screener, peer comparison, ratios, portfolio, the broker's research | A broker's research tool | Broker clients |
| [StockAnalyzer.lk](https://www.stockanalyzer.lk/), [StockDecision](https://www.stockdecision.com/), [StockNetwork](https://stocknetwork.net/), [CSE Tracker Pro](https://csetracker.lk/) | AI analysis, screeners, portfolio tracking, alerts | Variations of the above | Various |

**Implication:** another share screener with AI commentary would be the seventh. ZinvestIQ's ground is different:

1. **The whole picture for a Sri Lankan saver:** shares, government bonds and bills, and fixed deposits against
   inflation, side by side. No competitor reviewed covers government securities at all.
2. **Explained, not rated.** No signals, scores, stances or targets: what the numbers are, why they matter, and how to
   read them. This is also the legally safe ground (hard rule 4; signals like CSEdge's are exactly what the SEC's
   investment-advice rules concern).
3. **Numbers you can check.** Every figure comes from a published method with a version number, point-in-time.
   Returns include dividends and share changes (total return), which the CSE's own prices do not.
4. **Disclosures made readable:** directors' dealings with the notice linked, ownership with look-through to the
   group, interim-report figures checked against the report.

## 4. How to present it (design evidence)

- **Progressive disclosure, carefully.** Show the essentials first and reveal detail on request; it lowers cognitive
  load. Accordions suit content-heavy pages where a visitor needs only some sections. They hurt when the visitor needs
  most of the content or must compare across sections ([NN/g, Accordions on desktop](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/accordions-on-desktop/);
  [NN/g video](https://www.nngroup.com/videos/avoid-accordions/)). Headings must be short and scannable (design
  systems such as [Toyota](https://dx-playbook.toyota-europe.com/cds/components/accordion/)). Legal information stays
  visible.
  - **For ZinvestIQ:** every long page opens with a plain-language "at a glance" summary that is always visible. Each
    detailed section is collapsible, with the most-used one open, and its heading carries a one-line summary so a
    closed section still informs. There is an "open all" control and a list of jump links. Disclaimers are never
    collapsed.
- **Credibility comes from restraint.** Practitioner guides (not research) agree on calm, low-saturation palettes, with
  blues and greens reading as stable. Saturated red reads as alarm and loss; inconsistent styles and generic templates
  read as amateur ([The Financial Brand](https://thefinancialbrand.com/news/digital-marketing-banking/clickable-to-credible-how-design-drives-financial-loyalty-191899);
  [Flow Ninja](https://www.flow.ninja/blog/fintech-websites-trust-issues)). The often-quoted "94% of first impressions
  are design" figure could not be traced to its source and is not relied on.
  - **For ZinvestIQ:** drop the gradient hero and glow. Use paper-white and ink, one deep accent colour, muted green
    and red only for up and down, and editorial type (a serif for headings, a clean sans for numbers). Trust signals
    (sources, methods, "not advice") stay near the top, not only in the footer.
- **Show what paying adds.** People upgrade to reach full content and for clear value. Previewing premium work helps
  ([Adapty](https://adapty.io/blog/top-reasons-mobile-app-users-go-from-freemium-to-premium/);
  [Stripe](https://stripe.com/us/resources/more/freemium-business-model); [MISQ study](https://aisel.aisnet.org/misq/vol42/iss3/4)).
  - **For ZinvestIQ:** locked panels say what they would show and why it is useful, using a delayed or example view where
    the tier rules allow it (free_preview). The Plans page explains each tier by who it is for, not by listing models.

## 5. Decisions for the redesign (proposals; the owner decides)

1. **Promise:** "Sri Lanka's markets, explained." Shares, government bonds and bills, and savings in one place, in plain
   language, with every method published. No tips, signals or ratings.
2. **Front door by goal:** four starting paths ("I keep my money in fixed deposits", "I'm new to shares", "I already
   own shares", "I follow bonds and bills"). Each leads to a guide and the matching tool. These are learning paths,
   not advice.
3. **Every panel answers three questions:** what this shows, why it matters, how to read it; with related links to the
   connected tool and guide.
4. **Learn is the spine:** guides at three levels that match the tiers' tools (basics → going deeper → advanced). They
   are free to read, so education is never paywalled. Paid tiers add the live tools the advanced guides explain.
5. **Collapsible sections and jump links** on long pages; navigation menus grouped by market.
6. **Visual system:** restrained and editorial. The preview shows two alternative palettes so the owner and Janu can
   choose.
